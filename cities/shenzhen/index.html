Shenzhen, China — the city that went from fishing village to tech capital in a single generation — is now positioning itself as one of China's most dynamic medical tourism destinations. Located immediately next to Hong Kong, Shenzhen was the first Chinese city to feel Hong Kong's influence, and that extends to medicine: new drugs approved in Hong Kong often appear in Shenzhen faster than anywhere else in mainland China.

Shenzhen is also home to some of China's most modern hospital infrastructure — the city was built in the 1980s-2000s, which means its medical facilities are among the newest in the country. For patients seeking new drugs, immunotherapy trials, and cutting-edge treatments, Shenzhen's proximity to Hong Kong's pharmaceutical approvals gives it a unique advantage.

Why Medical Tourists Choose Shenzhen

Shenzhen occupies a unique position in China's medical landscape — newer infrastructure, faster drug approvals, and strong connections to Hong Kong's medical ecosystem. Patients come here for treatments and drugs that haven't yet reached other parts of mainland China.

While You Wait — Explore the City

Many procedures and diagnostic tests (MRI, CT, blood work) can be scheduled within 3–7 days. While you wait for results or pre-procedure assessments, you can explore the city. Your hospital liaison office can help plan sightseeing between appointments.

How to pay:

Most hospitals accept cash (RMB), UnionPay cards, and major credit cards. WeChat Pay and Alipay require a Chinese bank account. HKU Shenzhen Hospital accepts international insurance directly.

How to Schedule an Appointment

Contact hospitals directly or through a medical facilitator.

Option 1: Contact the hospital directly

  1. Find the international/liaison office contact in the Hospital section above
  2. Email or call to request an appointment with your diagnosis and medical summary
  3. Bring your passport to registration

Option 2: Through a medical facilitator

Facilitators familiar with Shenzhen's hospital network can help with appointment booking, drug information, and logistics. Fees typically $200–400.

⚠️ What to bring to your appointment:

  • Passport (required)
  • All previous medical records, imaging CDs, and pathology reports
  • List of current medications
  • Any prior treatment records (especially immunotherapy or targeted therapy records)

Getting to Shenzhen from the US

Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport (SZX) is a major hub with growing international connections. There are no direct flights from the US — most travelers connect via Hong Kong, Seoul, Tokyo, or Vancouver.

RouteFlight DurationRound-Trip Economy
LAX → SZX (via HKG/ICN)~16–19 hours total$700–1,300
SFO → SZX (via ICN/HKG)~15–18 hours total$650–1,200
SEA → SZX (via Tokyo)~14–17 hours total$650–1,200

From the airport to the city:

  • Metro Line 11 (地铁11号线) — Connects SZX to Futian and Luohu districts. ¥25–40 to central stations. Allow 45–60 minutes.
  • Taxi — ¥100–180 to central Shenzhen. About 40–60 minutes depending on traffic.
  • Didi — Request via app with data; cheaper than taxis.

Visa:

US citizens need a tourist visa (L visa). Apply at the Chinese Visa Application Service Center. Processing: 4–5 business days. Cost: $140. Note: Shenzhen also has a 144-hour visa-free transit policy for many nationalities arriving via certain ports.

Alternative: Via Hong Kong:

Many patients fly into Hong Kong International Airport (HKG) and cross into Shenzhen by taxi, bus, or the MTR metro (about 45–60 minutes from HKG to Shenzhen city center). Hong Kong visa requirements differ from mainland China — check before choosing this route.

Money & Payments

Currency: Chinese Yuan (RMB / ¥). Same currency used throughout mainland China.

  • Cash: RMB cash works everywhere
  • Cards: Visa and Mastercard accepted at hospitals, hotels, and upscale restaurants
  • WeChat Pay & Alipay: Most convenient — Chinese bank account needed for full features

Exchange money:

  • Best rates: ATM withdrawals with US debit cards (Charles Schwab, Revolut, Wise — no foreign fees)
  • Airport: Available but poor rates
  • Banks: ICBC and China Merchants Bank near hospitals offer fair rates

Cost of living reference (Shenzhen):

  • Cheap meal: ¥20–40 ($3–6)
  • Mid-range restaurant: ¥80–200 ($12–30)
  • Subway: ¥3–10 ($0.50–1.50)
  • Taxi (per km): ¥2.5–4 ($0.40–0.60)

Staying Connected in China

Internet: China uses blocked internet. Install a VPN before arrival.

Before you leave the US:

  1. Install a VPN (ExpressVPN, NordVPN, etc.) on all devices and test it
  2. Download offline maps from Maps.me or Google Maps
  3. Download Google Translate with offline Chinese language pack
  4. Download WeChat and register your phone number

SIM cards at Shenzhen airport:

Buy a Chinese SIM card at SZX from China Mobile, China Unicom, or China Telecom. Tourist packages: ¥80–200 for 15–30 days with data. You'll need your passport to register.

Useful apps:

  • WeChat — essential
  • Didi — ride-hailing (works with foreign phone)
  • Pleco — Chinese dictionary
  • Maps.me — offline maps
  • Google Translate — offline language pack

Important Tips for First-Time Visitors

Ready to Get Started?

Shenzhen's modern hospitals and new drug access make it an excellent choice — especially for cancer immunotherapy and cutting-edge treatments.

Before you go:

  • Apply for your Chinese tourist visa (L visa)
  • Notify your bank you'll be in China
  • Install and test a VPN before arrival
  • Download WeChat and register your phone number
  • Bring all medical records, imaging CDs, and pathology reports
  • Save hospital addresses in Chinese characters
  • Bring a debit card with no foreign ATM fees
  • Research specific drugs or immunotherapies you're interested in — ask the hospital directly about availability

At the hospital:

  • Bring your passport — required
  • Arrive 30–60 minutes early
  • Keep all receipts and medical records
  • Ask about the latest drugs available for your condition

  • Keep all medical records and imaging CDs

About Shenzhen's Hospitals

Shenzhen's hospitals are among China's newest and most modern — built in the 2000s with the latest medical equipment and digital infrastructure. HKU Shenzhen Hospital, operated jointly with Hong Kong University, combines international standards with mainland pricing, offering procedures 40–60% below Hong Kong costs.

Shenzhen is particularly attractive for patients seeking new drugs and immunotherapies (CAR-T, PD-1 inhibitors) that may be available faster or at lower cost than in the West. Its proximity to Hong Kong also means easy access to Hong Kong hospitals if needed.
