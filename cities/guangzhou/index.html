Guangzhou, China — Southern China's largest city and its primary medical hub — is where patients from Southeast Asia, Africa, and the Middle East have sought treatment for decades, drawn by world-class care at a fraction of Western prices.

Known as the "South Gate of China," Guangzhou is the capital of Guangdong Province (population 120+ million) and hosts one of the country's most internationally diverse hospital networks. The city is especially renowned for gastroenterology and hepatology, ophthalmology, and advanced liver and kidney transplantation. Guangzhou's geographic position near Hong Kong and Macau also means its hospitals were among the first in China to adopt international standards.

Why Medical Tourists Choose Guangzhou

Guangzhou occupies a unique niche in China's medical landscape. While Beijing focuses on national-level research institutions and Shanghai on international patient comfort, Guangzhou excels in high-volume clinical care — particularly in digestive diseases, liver conditions, and eye disorders. Guangzhou's hospitals treat some of the highest patient volumes in the world, which means specialists here have unmatched experience in common and complex conditions alike.

How to pay:

Most hospitals accept cash (RMB), UnionPay cards, and major credit cards (Visa, Mastercard). WeChat Pay and Alipay are widely used but require a Chinese bank account.

How to Schedule an Appointment

You can contact Guangzhou's hospitals directly without a medical tourism agency.

Option 1: Contact the hospital directly (recommended)

  1. Find the international or liaison office contact in the Hospital section above
  2. Email or call to request an appointment — include your diagnosis, medical summary, and preferred dates
  3. Bring your passport to registration
  4. Arrive early on the day of your appointment

Option 2: Through a medical facilitator

Medical tourism facilitators operating in Guangzhou can help with appointment booking, translation, and logistics. Fees typically $200–400.

Option 3: Walk in

For diagnostic procedures (endoscopy, CT, MRI, blood work), many departments accept walk-ins. However, booking ahead is strongly recommended to avoid long queues.

⚠️ What to bring to your appointment:

  • Passport (required for all foreign patients)
  • Previous medical records in English if available
  • List of current medications
  • Imaging CDs (CT, MRI) — not just written reports
  • Cash or card for payment

Language tips:

  • Download Google Translate with offline Chinese language pack
  • Pleco dictionary for medical terminology
  • WeChat has built-in translation features
  • Most large hospitals have English-speaking coordinators in their international liaison offices

Getting to Guangzhou from the US

Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport (CAN) is one of China's busiest airports. There are no direct flights from the US — most travelers connect via Los Angeles, San Francisco, or Vancouver (Canada).

RouteFlight DurationRound-Trip Economy
LAX → CAN (via SFO/Vancouver)~15–18 hours total$700–1,300
SFO → CAN (via Seoul/Hong Kong)~15–17 hours total$650–1,200
SEA → CAN (via Tokyo)~14–16 hours total$650–1,200

*Prices vary by season and routing. Book 2–4 weeks ahead for best rates.

From the airport to the city:

  • Metro Line 3 (地铁3号线) — Direct connection from CAN to central Guangzhou. Runs until ~11pm. ¥7–12 to central stations. Fastest and cheapest option.
  • Taxi — ¥80–150 to central Guangzhou (about 30–45 minutes). Use the official taxi queue.
  • Didi — Request via app once you have data; often cheaper than taxis.

Visa:

US citizens need a tourist visa (L visa). Apply at the Chinese Visa Application Service Center. Processing: 4–5 business days. Cost: $140. Some nationalities may qualify for 72-hour or 144-hour visa-free transit through Guangzhou — check with your airline.

Money & Payments

Currency: Chinese Yuan (RMB / ¥)

  • Cash: RMB cash works everywhere
  • Cards: Visa and Mastercard accepted at most hospitals and hotels
  • WeChat Pay & Alipay: Most convenient for daily expenses — Chinese bank account recommended for full features

Exchange money:

  • Best rates: ATM withdrawals with US debit cards (Charles Schwab, Revolut, Wise — no foreign fees)
  • Airport exchange: Available but poor rates
  • Banks: ICBC and China Construction Bank near the hospitals offer fair exchange rates

💳 Before leaving the US:

Notify your bank you'll be in China to prevent fraud-based card blocks. Consider bringing a backup card. Also install a VPN before arrival — important apps like Google Translate and Gmail require it in China.

Cost of living reference (Guangzhou):

  • Cheap meal: ¥15–35 ($2–5)
  • Mid-range restaurant: ¥60–150 ($9–22)
  • Subway: ¥2–8 ($0.30–1.20)
  • Taxi (per km): ¥2.5–4 ($0.40–0.60)

Staying Connected in China

Internet: China uses blocked internet (no Google, YouTube, Facebook, WhatsApp). Install a VPN before arrival.

Before you leave the US:

  1. Install a VPN (ExpressVPN, NordVPN, etc.) on all devices and test it
  2. Download offline maps from Google Maps or Maps.me
  3. Download Google Translate with offline Chinese language pack

SIM cards at Guangzhou airport:

Buy a Chinese SIM card at CAN from China Mobile, China Unicom, or China Telecom. Tourist packages: ¥80–200 for 15–30 days with data.

Useful apps to download before arrival:

  • WeChat — essential for communication and payments
  • Didi — ride-hailing (works with foreign phone numbers)
  • Pleco — Chinese-English dictionary with camera translation
  • Google Translate — offline language pack
  • Maps.me — offline navigation

Important Tips for First-Time Visitors

Ready to Get Started?

Contact the hospital's international office directly. Guangzhou's hospitals are experienced with foreign patients and the process is straightforward.

Before you go:

  • Apply for your Chinese tourist visa (L visa)
  • Notify your bank you'll be in China
  • Install and test a VPN before arrival
  • Download WeChat and register your phone number
  • Translate medical records into English summaries
  • Bring imaging CDs (not just written reports)
  • Save hospital addresses in Chinese characters
  • Bring a debit card with no foreign ATM fees
  • Bring prescription medications from home

At the hospital:

  • Bring your passport — required for all foreign patient registrations
  • Arrive early; hospitals can be very crowded
  • Keep all medical receipts and records
  • Ask for English-language summaries of your treatment plan

🚨 Emergencies:

  • Ambulance: 120
  • Police: 110
  • Save your hotel address in Chinese characters

After you return home:

  • Keep all medical records and imaging CDs from your trip
  • Follow up with your US doctor using the Chinese hospital's records
  • Contact the hospital directly for any follow-up needs
