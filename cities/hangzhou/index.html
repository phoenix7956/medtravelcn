Hangzhou, China — home to Alibaba and China's most thriving tech ecosystem — has quietly become one of the country's most innovative medical destinations. The city that pioneered "internet hospitals" (online medical consultations now fully legal in China) offers something no other Chinese city can: world-class care combined with the most advanced digital health infrastructure in the country.

Hangzhou is Zhejiang Province's capital (65 million people) and one of China's most beautiful cities — famous for West Lake, tea gardens, and a high quality of life that attracts both tech talent and increasingly, medical tourists. Its hospitals are among the most digitally advanced in China, with seamless online appointment booking, cloud-based medical records, and telemedicine capabilities that make follow-up care from overseas remarkably convenient.

Why Medical Tourists Choose Hangzhou

Hangzhou's distinct advantage is its combination of excellent hospitals and China's most developed digital health infrastructure. If you need ongoing care or follow-up after returning home, Hangzhou's "internet hospitals" allow you to maintain a relationship with your Chinese doctors via online consultations.

While You Wait — Explore Hangzhou

Hangzhou is one of China's most scenic cities. West Lake (Xihu), a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is right in the center of town — walking around it costs nothing. The famous tea villages of Longjing (Longjing Village) are a short bus ride away. Hangzhou's internet hospitals make follow-up consultations from your hotel easy while you wait.

  • West Lake — walk or bike thecauseway, no entry fee needed
  • Leifeng Pagoda — historic pagoda overlooking the lake, ¥45
  • Longjing Tea Village — see how dragon well tea is picked and brewed
  • Hangzhou's food scene — famous for West Lake vinegar fish, Longjing shrimp, and dongpo pork

How to pay:

Most hospitals accept cash (RMB), UnionPay cards, and major credit cards. WeChat Pay and Alipay are widely used for daily expenses. The internet hospital platform accepts international credit cards for online consultations.

How to Schedule an Appointment

Option 1: Contact the hospital's international office directly

  1. Find the international/liaison contact in the Hospital section above
  2. Email or call to request an appointment — include diagnosis and medical summary
  3. For online consultations: ask about the hospital's "internet hospital" platform for remote follow-up
  4. Bring your passport to registration

Option 2: Book online via the hospital's WeChat service

Most Hangzhou hospitals have WeChat public accounts where you can book appointments, view results, and access the internet hospital feature. Download WeChat before you arrive.

⚠️ What to bring to your appointment:

  • Passport (required)
  • All previous medical records and imaging CDs
  • List of current medications
  • Ask about the hospital's internet hospital / telemedicine platform for follow-up

Language tips:

  • Download Google Translate with offline Chinese language pack
  • Pleco dictionary for medical terminology
  • WeChat has built-in translation features
  • Most international offices provide English coordinators

Getting to Hangzhou from the US

Hangzhou Xiaoshan International Airport (HGH) has limited direct US flights. Most travelers connect through Shanghai (PVG), Seoul (ICN), or Tokyo (NRT). Hangzhou is also reachable via high-speed train from Shanghai Pudong Airport (PVG) in about 60 minutes.

RouteFlight DurationRound-Trip Economy
LAX → HGH (via ICN/NRT)~14–17 hours total$650–1,200
LAX → PVG → HGH (train)~14 + 1h train$700–1,300
SFO → HGH (via Seoul)~14–16 hours total$600–1,100

From the airport to the city:

  • Metro Line 1 (地铁1号线) — Connects Xiaoshan Airport to central Hangzhou (about 40 minutes to East Lake/West Lake area). ¥6–10.
  • Taxi — ¥80–150 to central Hangzhou. About 30–50 minutes.
  • Didi — Request via app once you have data; often cheaper than taxis.

Visa:

US citizens need a tourist visa (L visa). Apply at the Chinese Visa Application Service Center. Processing: 4–5 business days. Cost: $140. Note: Hangzhou also participates in the 144-hour visa-free transit policy for many nationalities.

From Shanghai:

If flying into Shanghai (PVG), take the high-speed G-train from Shanghai Hongqiao station to Hangzhou East station (~45–60 minutes, ¥73). Or take the metro from PVG to Hongqiao first (~90 minutes).

Staying Connected in China

Internet: China uses blocked internet. Install a VPN before arrival.

Before you leave the US:

  1. Install a VPN (ExpressVPN, NordVPN, etc.) on all devices
  2. Download offline maps from Maps.me or Google Maps
  3. Download Google Translate with offline Chinese language pack
  4. Download WeChat and register your phone number — essential for hospital appointment booking and internet hospital access

SIM cards at Hangzhou airport:

Buy a Chinese SIM card at HGH from China Mobile, China Unicom, or China Telecom. Tourist packages: ¥80–200 for 15–30 days with data. Passport required.

Useful apps:

  • WeChat — essential (hospital booking, payments, internet hospital)
  • Didi — ride-hailing (works with foreign phone)
  • Pleco — Chinese dictionary
  • Google Translate — offline language pack
  • Maps.me — offline maps

Important Tips for First-Time Visitors

Ready to Get Started?

Hangzhou's combination of excellent hospitals and digital health infrastructure makes it ideal for ongoing care. Ask about the internet hospital platform when you contact the hospital.

Before you go:

  • Apply for your Chinese tourist visa (L visa)
  • Notify your bank you'll be in China
  • Install and test a VPN before arrival
  • Download WeChat and register your phone number
  • Ask about the hospital's "internet hospital" telemedicine platform for post-treatment follow-up
  • Bring all medical records, imaging CDs, and pathology reports
  • Save hospital addresses in Chinese characters
  • Bring a debit card with no foreign ATM fees

At the hospital:

  • Bring your passport — required
  • Ask how to access the internet hospital platform for follow-up consultations from home
  • Keep all receipts and medical records
  • Ask for English-language summaries of your treatment plan

🚨 Emergencies:

  • Ambulance: 120
  • Police: 110

After you return home:

  • Keep all medical records and imaging CDs
  • Use the hospital's internet hospital platform for follow-up consultations — most consultations cost $15–50 via the online platform
  • Follow up with your US doctor using the Chinese hospital's records

About Hangzhou's Hospitals

Hangzhou is China's leading city for digital health and telemedicine innovation. Zhejiang University School of Medicine affiliated hospitals are among China's most digitally advanced — offering online consultations, electronic medical records, and appointment systems that rival the best in the world.

The city's "internet hospitals" allow international patients to connect with specialists for pre-travel consultations from abroad, reducing the need for multiple trips. Sir Run Run Shaw Hospital is a model for efficiency and patient experience.
