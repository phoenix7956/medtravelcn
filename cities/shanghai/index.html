When American patients need medical procedures they can't afford or can't wait for at home, more and more are discovering that Shanghai, China offers world-class healthcare at a fraction of the cost.

Shanghai is China's largest city (24 million people), a global financial hub, and home to some of the country's most advanced hospitals. The city has decades of experience serving international patients and has recently emerged as one of Asia's leading medical tourism destinations — treating patients from 90+ countries in 2025 alone.

While You Wait — Explore Shanghai

Many procedures and diagnostic tests (MRI, CT, blood work) can be scheduled within 3–7 days. While you wait for results or pre-procedure assessments, you don't need to sit in a hotel — Shanghai is one of the world's most accessible cities.

  • The Bund — walk along the waterfront at night, lit up spectacularly
  • Yu Garden — 400-year-old classical garden in the old city
  • Nanjing Road — major shopping street, from bargain to luxury
  • Jing'an Temple — right in central Shanghai, centuries old
  • French Concession — tree-lined streets, cafes, boutique shops
  • Shanghai Tower — world's second tallest building, glass skywalk

All major hospitals are accessible by metro. Use Maps.me or Google Translate to navigate. Your hospital liaison office can help with trip planning between appointments.

How to pay:

Most hospitals accept cash (RMB), UnionPay debit cards, and major credit cards (Visa, Mastercard). WeChat Pay and Alipay require a Chinese bank account — see the Money section below.

How to Schedule an Appointment

You do NOT need to go through a medical tourism agency. Any patient can contact hospitals directly.

Option 1: Contact the hospital directly (recommended)

  1. Find the hospital's international or liaison office phone number (listed in the Hospital section above)
  2. Call or email to request an appointment
  3. Bring your passport — foreign patients must show ID at registration
  4. Arrive early on the day of your appointment (hospitals can be crowded)

Option 2: Use the hospital's WeChat public account

  1. Download WeChat before you arrive in China
  2. Search for the hospital's official account name (ask the hospital via email/phone)
  3. Follow the account and use the appointment booking feature

Option 3: Walk in

For simpler procedures (CT, MRI, blood work), you can often walk into the radiology or lab department without an appointment. However, booking ahead is strongly recommended to avoid waiting.

⚠️ What to bring to your appointment:

  • Passport (required for all foreign patients)
  • Any previous medical records you have (in English if possible)
  • List of current medications
  • Cash or card for payment

Language tips:

  • Download Google Translate or Pleco (Chinese dictionary) before you arrive
  • Most large hospitals have staff who can communicate in basic English at international liaison desks
  • Download WeChat — it has a built-in translation feature
  • Chrome browser translates Chinese web pages automatically

From the airport to the city:

  • Shanghai Pudong Airport (PVG) — Located in Pudong District, ~40 km from city center. Metro Line 2 connects to central Shanghai (approx. ¥8, ~1 hour). Taxi: ¥150–220 to central areas.
  • Shanghai Hongqiao Airport (SHA) — Located in Changning District, closer to central Shanghai. Metro Lines 2/10/17 connect directly. Taxi: ¥40–80 to central areas.

Visa:

Most passport holders need a tourist visa (L visa) to enter China. You can apply at the Chinese Visa Application Service Center. Processing time: typically 4–5 business days. Cost varies by country and processing speed.

Many cities also offer 144-hour visa-free transit — if you're connecting through Shanghai to another country, you may qualify. Check with your airline or the visa center.

Money & Payments

Currency: Chinese Yuan (RMB / ¥)

  • Cash: RMB cash works everywhere, including small restaurants and markets
  • Cards: Visa and Mastercard are accepted at most hotels, large hospitals, and upscale restaurants
  • WeChat Pay & Alipay: The most convenient for everything else — but require a Chinese bank account to activate

Exchange money:

  • Best rates: Withdraw RMB from an ATM using your US debit card (Charles Schwab, Revolut, etc. have no foreign fees)
  • Airport exchange: Available but usually gives poor rates
  • Banks: Can exchange cash with passport

💳 WeChat Pay & Alipay — How to set up as a foreigner:

WeChat Pay: Download WeChat and register with your phone number. Most foreign users need a Chinese bank account to link. Some hotels can help set up a temporary payment account. As a workaround: use WeChat for communication only, pay with cash or card.

Alipay: Same requirements as WeChat Pay — Chinese bank account needed for full features. Foreign credit cards (Visa/Mastercard) can be linked to some Alipay accounts for small purchases.

Tip: Before you leave the US, notify your bank that you'll be in China to avoid having your card blocked for fraud protection.

Staying Connected in China

Internet: China uses blocked internet (no Google, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp). Plan ahead:

Before you leave the US:

  1. Sign up for a VPN service (ExpressVPN, NordVPN, etc.) — install it on your phone and laptop before you arrive
  2. Download offline maps from Google Maps or Maps.me — they work without internet
  3. Download any translation apps

In China — SIM cards:

Buy a Chinese SIM card at the airport upon arrival:

  • China Mobile, China Unicom, and China Telecom all offer tourist SIM cards
  • Cost: ¥50–200 for 10–30 days with data packages
  • You'll need your passport to register the SIM

Recommended data packages (tourist SIM):

  • 10 GB / 15 days: approx. ¥100 ($14)
  • 20 GB / 30 days: approx. ¥150 ($21)

Useful apps to download before arrival:

  • WeChat — communication + translation
  • Alipay — payments
  • Didi — ride-hailing (works with foreign phone numbers now)
  • Pleco — Chinese-English dictionary with camera translation
  • Google Translate — offline Chinese language pack
  • Maps.me or Booking.com — hotels and navigation

Important Tips for First-Time Visitors

Ready to Get Started?

You don't need a medical tourism agency. Contact the hospital directly — it's faster, cheaper, and just as easy.

Before you go:

  • Get a Chinese tourist visa (L visa) from the Chinese visa center
  • Tell your bank you'll be in China (avoid card blocks)
  • Sign up for a VPN before you arrive
  • Download Google Translate + offline Chinese language pack
  • Download WeChat and register your phone number
  • Save hospital addresses in Chinese (show them to taxi drivers)
  • Bring USD cash to exchange at bank ATMs (better rates than airports)
  • Bring any prescription medications you need from home
  • Check if your regular medications are legal to bring to China

At the hospital:

  • Bring your passport — required for all foreign patients
  • Arrive early (hospitals get very crowded)
  • Keep all receipts for your records
  • Ask if the hospital has an English-speaking liaison office

🚨 Emergencies:

  • Emergency number in China: 120 (ambulance)
  • Police: 110
  • Save your hotel's address in Chinese on your phone — show it to anyone who needs to help you

After you return home:

  • Keep all medical records and receipts from your trip
  • Follow up with your regular doctor in the US
  • If you need to return for follow-up treatment, contact the hospital directly via email
