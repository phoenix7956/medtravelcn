When American patients need treatment for complex cancers, rare diseases, or conditions that require the absolute best specialists, Beijing, China is increasingly the answer — at a fraction of US prices.

Beijing is China's political capital and home to the country's most prestigious medical institutions. The national government funds top hospitals here, attracting the best doctors from across China. Peking Union Medical College Hospital, the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences Cancer Hospital (东肿), and PLA General Hospital (301) represent the pinnacle of Chinese medicine — treating heads of state and complex cases for over a century.

Why Medical Tourists Choose Beijing

Beijing's hospitals are the most renowned in China for complex, serious, and rare conditions. Where Shanghai leads in international comfort, Beijing leads in medical depth — hosting the national cancer center, the largest liver transplant program, and specialists who treat the rarest diseases in the country.

While You Wait — Explore the City

Many procedures and diagnostic tests (MRI, CT, blood work) can be scheduled within 3–7 days. While you wait for results or pre-procedure assessments, you can explore the city. Your hospital liaison office can help plan sightseeing between appointments.

How to pay:

Most hospitals accept cash (RMB), UnionPay cards, and major credit cards (Visa, Mastercard). WeChat Pay and Alipay require a Chinese bank account. See the Money section below.

How to Schedule an Appointment

You can contact Beijing's top hospitals directly without using a medical tourism agency.

Option 1: Contact the hospital's international office directly (recommended)

  1. Find the international liaison office phone/email in the Hospital section above
  2. Email or call to request an appointment — include your diagnosis, medical history summary, and preferred dates
  3. Bring your passport to registration
  4. Request a medical records transfer from any previous Chinese hospitals you've visited

Option 2: Through a medical facilitator

If you prefer English-language coordination, companies like Maye-stone, Paiter, and similar medical tourism services can help arrange appointments, translation, and hospital logistics in Beijing. Fees typically range $200–500 for this service.

Option 3: Walk in for consultations

For initial consultations at the international desk (without a pre-booked appointment), you can often arrive early morning and register on a first-come basis. However, booking ahead is strongly recommended for specific specialists.

⚠️ What to bring to your appointment:

  • Passport (required)
  • All previous medical records, imaging CDs, and pathology reports (in English if possible)
  • List of current medications
  • Any imaging already done — bring CDs, not just written reports

Language tips:

  • Download Google Translate with an offline Chinese language pack
  • Pleco dictionary is the best tool for medical Chinese terminology
  • Most international offices provide basic translation during consultations at no extra charge
  • WeChat has a built-in translation feature for written Chinese → English

Getting to Beijing from the US

Beijing Capital International Airport (PEK) is one of the world's busiest airports, with direct flights from Los Angeles (~13 hours), San Francisco, Seattle, Detroit, and New York (via connecting flights).

Departure CityFlight DurationRound-Trip Economy
Los Angeles (LAX)~13 hours (direct)$700–1,400
San Francisco (SFO)~13 hours (direct)$700–1,400
Seattle (SEA)~12 hours (direct)$650–1,300
New York (JFK)~14 hours (direct)$800–1,500

*Prices vary by season. Book 2–4 weeks ahead for best rates.

From the airport to the city:

  • Airport Express Line (机场快轨) — ¥25 one way, connects PEK to Dongzhimen station in ~35 minutes. Runs 6:20am–11:20pm.
  • Metro Line 4 — Connects to central Beijing via multiple transfer points. Budget option but can be crowded with luggage.
  • Taxi — ¥100–180 to central Beijing (about 30–45 minutes depending on traffic). Use the official taxi queue outside Terminal 3.
  • Didi (ride-hail) — Request via the Didi app once you have data; often cheaper than taxis.

Visa:

US citizens need a tourist visa (L visa). Apply at the Chinese Visa Application Service Center. Processing: 4–5 business days. Cost: $140. For medical treatment, you may also request an M visa (medical visit) with a letter from the Chinese hospital.

Money & Payments

Currency: Chinese Yuan (RMB / ¥)

  • Cash: RMB cash works everywhere including hospitals, restaurants, and markets
  • Cards: Visa and Mastercard accepted at most large hospitals, hotels, and upscale restaurants
  • WeChat Pay & Alipay: Most convenient for daily expenses — but require a Chinese bank account to link foreign cards (some hospitals accept WeChat for payment with foreign cards, check in advance)

Exchange money:

  • Best rates: Withdraw RMB from an ATM using a US debit card (Charles Schwab, Revolut, Wise — no foreign fees)
  • Airport exchange: Available at PEK but rates are poor
  • Banks: ICBC, Bank of China, and ABC exchange cash with a passport at reasonable rates

💳 WeChat Pay & Alipay — How to set up as a foreigner:

WeChat Pay: Download WeChat, register with your phone number. As of 2024, some foreign credit cards (Visa/Mastercard) can be linked — check WeChat settings. For full features, a Chinese bank account is still most reliable.

Alipay: Same situation — foreign card linking is improving but not yet universal. Bring sufficient RMB cash as backup.

Tip: Before you leave the US, notify your bank you'll be in China to prevent fraud-based card blocks.

Staying Connected in China

Internet: China uses blocked internet. You will NOT have access to Google, YouTube, Facebook, WhatsApp, or many other apps without a VPN.

Before you leave the US:

  1. Install a VPN (ExpressVPN, NordVPN, or VyprVPN) on your phone and laptop — set it up and test it before you arrive
  2. Download offline maps from Google Maps or Maps.me — they work without internet
  3. Download translation apps with offline Chinese packs
  4. Save important addresses (hospital, hotel) as screenshots in your photo gallery

SIM cards at Beijing airport:

Buy a Chinese SIM card at PEK upon arrival from China Mobile, China Unicom, or China Telecom:

  • China Mobile: Most widespread coverage; tourist SIM ¥50–200 for data packages
  • China Unicom: Good for international travelers; similar pricing
  • You'll need your passport to register the SIM card

Recommended data packages (tourist SIM):

  • 15 GB / 30 days: approx. ¥100–150 ($14–21)
  • 30 GB / 30 days: approx. ¥200 ($28)

Useful apps to download before arrival:

  • WeChat — essential for communication, payments, and translation
  • Didi — China's main ride-hailing app (works with foreign phone numbers)
  • Pleco — best Chinese-English dictionary with camera OCR
  • Google Translate — with offline Chinese language pack
  • Maps.me — offline navigation maps
  • Feiyu VPN or your preferred VPN service

Important Tips for First-Time Visitors

Ready to Get Started?

Contact the hospital's international office directly — it's the most reliable way to arrange your visit to Beijing.

Before you go:

  • Apply for your Chinese tourist visa (L or M visa) — allow 2–3 weeks processing time
  • Notify your US bank that you'll be in China (prevent card blocks)
  • Install and test a VPN before you arrive in China
  • Download WeChat and register your phone number
  • Translate and save your medical records into English summaries
  • Bring all imaging CDs (CT, MRI, PET) — not just written reports
  • Save hospital addresses in Chinese on your phone (show to taxi drivers)
  • Exchange USD for RMB or bring a debit card with no foreign ATM fees
  • Bring prescription medications from home (check China's import regulations)

At the hospital:

  • Bring your passport — required for all foreign patient registrations
  • Arrive 30–60 minutes before your appointment time
  • Keep all medical receipts and discharge summaries for your records
  • Ask for English-language summaries of your treatment plan

🚨 Emergencies:

  • Emergency number in China: 120 (ambulance)
  • Police: 110
  • Save your hotel's address in Chinese characters on your phone — show it to anyone who needs to help you

After you return home:

  • Keep all medical records, imaging CDs, and receipts from your trip
  • Follow up with your US doctor with the Chinese hospital's records and imaging
  • If you need follow-up treatment, contact the Beijing hospital's international office directly via email

About Beijing's Hospitals

Beijing is home to China's most prestigious medical institutions. Peking Union Medical College Hospital (PUMCH), founded in 1906, serves as the national referral center for rare and complex diseases, drawing patients from across Asia and the world. The Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences Cancer Hospital (CAMS Cancer Hospital, known as "Dongzhong") is China's leading cancer treatment center.

Beijing's hospitals have the longest history of serving international patients, with dedicated liaison offices, English documentation support, and protocols designed for foreign visitors. Many specialists have trained abroad and hold international certifications.
