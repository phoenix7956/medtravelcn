Is it safe to get medical treatment in China?

Yes — many Chinese hospitals are among the best in the world, particularly in major cities like Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Chengdu. Top hospitals like Peking Union Medical College Hospital, Ruijin Hospital, and West China Hospital serve international patients with internationally trained doctors, JCI accreditation, and dedicated foreign patient departments.

As with any medical travel, do your research, choose a reputable hospital with an international patient center, and consider using a medical liaison service for guidance.

Do I need a visa for medical treatment in China?

Yes. A tourist visa (L visa) is typically sufficient for most medical treatments. Some hospitals can provide a medical invitation letter which helps with the visa application. For longer-term treatments or stays over 30 days, you may need a Medical Stay visa (M visa) — your hospital can advise.

Tip: Check the Chinese embassy website for the latest visa requirements for your country.

Will there be a language barrier?

Most top hospitals have international patient departments with English-speaking staff. We also recommend using a medical liaison service — they can accompany you to appointments, translate medical documents, and help with hospital paperwork.

Bring a translation app (like Google Translate with offline Chinese packages) for everyday situations outside the hospital.

How do I pay for treatment?

Most hospitals accept:

  • International credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) — usually with a 2–3% foreign transaction fee
  • Bank transfers (wire transfer to hospital account, arranged in advance)
  • Cash (CNY) — some smaller clinics prefer this
  • Alipay / WeChat Pay — if you have a Chinese bank account or phone number

Confirm payment methods with your hospital before arrival. Deposits are usually required upfront.

How do I book an appointment?

Options:

  1. Directly via the hospital's website — most major hospitals have English-language booking forms or WeChat/mini-program booking
  2. Through the hospital's international patient center — email or call their international department
  3. Through us — send us a message with your procedure, preferred city, and timeline, and we'll help connect you

We recommend booking at least 2–4 weeks in advance for specialist consultations and elective procedures.

What should I bring?
  • Passport (valid 6+ months)
  • All medical records in English or Chinese (reports, scans, lab results)
  • List of current medications
  • Insurance documents (if applicable)
  • Credit/debit card + some cash in CNY
  • Adaptor plug (China uses Type A/I plugs, 220V)
  • Toiletries and any specific medications you need (some may not be available)
What about follow-up care after I return home?

Ask your Chinese hospital for:

  • Complete medical records in English (discharge summary, operative reports, prescriptions)
  • Digital copies of all imaging/scans on USB or CD
  • A detailed follow-up care plan
  • Contact details of your treating physician for remote consultation if needed

Share these with your local doctor back home so they can continue your care seamlessly.

Can I get a medical evacuation if something goes wrong?

For emergencies, call 120 (China's ambulance/emergency number). Major cities have internationally accredited hospitals with emergency departments.

We strongly recommend purchasing international health insurance that covers medical evacuation before your trip, especially for elective procedures.

How long do I need to stay for a procedure?

Depends on the procedure:

  • Minor procedures / diagnostics (MRI, endoscopy, eye surgery): 2–7 days
  • Major surgery (knee replacement, cardiac): 2–4 weeks including recovery in-country
  • Dental implants: May require 2 visits (4–6 weeks apart) for implant + crown

Your surgeon can give you a estimated timeline after your initial consultation.

Is my medical information kept confidential?

Chinese hospitals maintain strict patient confidentiality. Your medical records and personal information are protected under Chinese privacy laws. We also do not share any user information with third parties.
